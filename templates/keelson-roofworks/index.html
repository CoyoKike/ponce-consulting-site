How we build a roof

  1. 01Framing

    Every truss strapped to the wall.

    Before anything goes on top, we check each truss and the hurricane straps that tie it into the bond beam. Rusted or missing straps get replaced, and all of it goes in your photo log.

    Straps
    On every truss
    Spacing
    24 in. on center
    Photo log
    From day one

  2. 02Decking

    Solid wood, nailed like it matters.

    Soft or delaminated plywood is cut out and replaced, never covered over. Then the whole deck is re-nailed with 8d ring-shank nails at 6 inches on center, the way Florida code asks for it.

    Sheathing
    19/32 in. plywood
    Nails
    8d ring-shank
    Pattern
    6 in. on center

  3. 03Underlayment

    Sealed before the first shingle.

    A self-adhered secondary water barrier covers the entire deck, lapped and rolled tight. If a storm ever strips shingles off, the house stays dry underneath. Most homes are dried in by the end of the first day.

    Membrane
    Peel-and-stick
    Coverage
    Entire deck
    Laps
    3 in. side, 6 in. end

  4. 04Shingles

    Laid course by course, eave to ridge.

    Starter strip on every edge, six nails in every shingle, offsets checked as we go up. Metal and tile roofs are fastened to the engineered pattern for your address instead.

    Nails
    6 per shingle
    Wind rating
    130 to 150 mph
    Starter
    All edges

  5. 05Edges

    Drip edge, gutters, hip and ridge cap.

    Metal drip edge on every eave and rake, new flashing at walls and vents, gutters hung on the new fascia, caps set last. Most leaks we get called for start at an edge someone skipped.

    Drip edge
    Every edge
    Flashing
    All new
    Gutters
    6 in. seamless

  6. 06Inspection

    Inspected, signed off, on paper.

    The city inspects the nailing and the finished roof. We close the permit and send you the photos, the warranty and a wind mitigation report you can hand to your insurance company.

Services

Three kinds of roof. One way of building them.

01

Architectural shingle

The most common roof in Tampa Bay and the quickest to replace. Dimensional shingles over a full peel-and-stick underlayment, with sealed starter strips at every edge. Most homes are torn off and dried in the same day.

Typical job
2 to 3 days
Wind rating
130 to 150 mph

02

Standing-seam metal

Concealed-fastener panels with mechanically seamed ribs, roll-formed to length on site so there are no end laps. Aluminum near salt water, steel inland, and a reflective finish that takes the edge off August.

Panels
24 gauge, 16 in. wide
Near the beach
.032 aluminum

03

Clay and concrete tile

The heaviest roof and the longest lasting. Tile is set with foam adhesive or screws to the engineered pattern, over two layers of underlayment. We also replace cracked tile and re-set loose hips without disturbing the rest.

Set with
Foam or screws
Underlayment
Two layers

  • Roof repair

    Leaks, lifted shingles, cracked tile, failed pipe boots. Every repair is photographed before and after, and if the roof is past repairing we tell you plainly.

  • Storm inspection and insurance claims

    After a named storm we document every slope with dated photos, measurements and a written report your adjuster can work from, and we meet the adjuster on the roof if you want us there.

  • Wind mitigation

    Our licensed inspector checks deck nailing, roof-to-wall connections and opening protection, and fills out the state form (OIR-B1-1802). It often lowers the windstorm part of your premium.

  • Gutters

    Seamless 6-inch aluminum gutters with oversized downspouts, sized for a summer afternoon downpour and pitched so the water leaves the house fast.
