Waterfronts

Every shoreline has a temper.

What matters on the water never makes the listing: bridge heights, depth at low tide, whether you face sunrise or sunset. Here is how I read each one.

  1. 01

    Venetian, Star & Sunset Islands

    Lots with water on three sides and the skyline in front. The Venetian Causeway drawbridges limit masts; for a tall sailboat, Star Island is the answer.

    Faces
    Sunset over downtown
    Boat
    Up to 120 ft on Star

  2. 02

    North Bay Road & La Gorce

    Miami Beach's most sought-after bayfront street. 1930s houses next to new builds; read the preservation rules closely before you make an offer.

    Faces
    West, across the bay
    Boat
    No fixed bridges to the bay

  3. 03

    Coconut Grove & Cocoplum

    Oak shade, sailing clubs and gated private canals. Water runs shallow in places, so I always order a depth survey before contract.

    Faces
    Sunrise over the bay
    Boat
    Built for sailors

  4. 04

    Key Biscayne

    An island with its own school, its own police and one bridge. Oceanfront houses are scarce; the bayside ones put Stiltsville and the reef minutes away.

    Faces
    Ocean or bay
    Boat
    The reef in 20 minutes

  5. 05

    Sunny Isles & Golden Beach

    Hotel-serviced towers in Sunny Isles; in Golden Beach, private beach houses on a road closed to through traffic. Two opposite ways to live on the Atlantic.

    Faces
    Sunrise over the Atlantic
    Boat
    Out through Haulover
