The story

Corn first,
then the fire

Every day starts with corn. Four heirloom varieties: blue, red, white and yellow.

It soaks overnight in lime and water. That is nixtamal. At dawn we grind it on stone and press each tortilla to order.

Salsas are made in a molcajete of volcanic rock. Moles simmer for two days in clay from Oaxaca.

No gas. Oak for heat, mesquite for smoke, ocote to start the fire.

The corn comes from nine milpa families in Oaxaca and Puebla. We pay them before the harvest, and their names are on the menu.

From wood to ash

One fire,
four stages

01 / 04
01

Wood

Oak from Puebla, split and dried for a year. Mesquite for the fish. Ocote to light the day.

02

Flame

Lit at 2 PM. High flame chars the chiles and puffs the tortillas on the clay comal.

03

Ember

By dinner the fire settles. Octopus and sweet potato are buried in the coals until the skin cracks.

04

Ash

Sifted at closing and mixed with sea salt from Colima. It seasons the first course the next night.
